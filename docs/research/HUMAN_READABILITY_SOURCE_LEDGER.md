# HUMAN_READABILITY_SOURCE_LEDGER.md

Source verification for
`docs/research/HUMAN_READABILITY_AND_VISUAL_EXPOSITION_PROTOCOL.md`,
under `AGENTS.md` rule 1 (CITE BEFORE YOU CODE).

**Verification date:** 2026-08-30.

**What counts as opened here.** A source is `VERIFIED` only when the
publisher's own deposited text for that article was retrieved and read in this
session, and a short passage from it is recorded below. Crossref *metadata*
(authors, title, venue, year, pages, DOI) was checked for every item, but
**metadata verification is not content verification** and does not by itself
license a source-to-rule mapping.

Anything not `VERIFIED` carries **no rule mapping** in the protocol. It is
listed as contextual reading only.

|---|---|
| Title | Learning from Examples: Instructional Principles from the Worked Examples Research |

### 2. Nathan & Petrosino (2003)

| field | value |
|---|---|
| Title | Expert Blind Spot Among Preservice Teachers |
| Venue | *American Educational Research Journal* 40(4), 905–928 |
| DOI | `10.3102/00028312040004905` |
| Opened | 2026-08-30, publisher-deposited abstract via the Crossref content API |
| Passage (10 words) | "This view is in contrast with students' actual performance patterns." |
| Supports | §14 and the Stage 7 reader referee: subject-matter expertise systematically distorts an expert's judgement of what a less-immersed reader will find hard |

| field | value |
|---|---|

## Standing caveat

None of the protocol's rules depends on these sources. The rules were derived
from a concrete project failure — an external mathematician found Paper 4's
setup and terminology harder to enter than necessary — and the literature is
recorded as the adjacent tradition, not as evidence.

The three verified items concern learners, preservice teachers, and proof
validation behaviour. **They do not establish an optimal format for a research
paper in combinatorics**, and the protocol must never be cited as if they did.
