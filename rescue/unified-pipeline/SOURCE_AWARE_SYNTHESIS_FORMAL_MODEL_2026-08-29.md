# SOURCE-AWARE SYNTHESIS FORMAL MODEL
**Date:** 2026-08-29

## 1. The Morphism Problem
Let Gamma be a finite source alphabet, typically of small cardinality.
Let x be an infinite source word (the controller or template), generated by a known mechanism (e.g., a square-free morphism like h6).
Let Sigma be the target alphabet, typically {a, b, c}.
Let L be a fixed block length.

A uniform morphism / block assignment is a mapping:
G : Gamma -> Sigma^L

The target object is the infinite word:
W = G(x)

## 2. Synthesis Requirements
For W to be a valid aa2fr word (or similar ternary square-free variant), the block assignment G must simultaneously satisfy the following mathematically disjoint conditions:

### A. Local Finite-Factor Safety
For a declared local half-period bound H = 2L - 1:
Every finite factor (substring) of W of length 2K with K <= H must not be an abelian square.
This condition is entirely finite and local. It depends only on the mapping G applied to the specific finite set of source factors (substrings) that actually occur in x.

### B. Long-Period Avoidance (K >= 2L)
No abelian square of half-period K >= 2L may occur in W.
This condition is verified algebraically:
1. G maps the factors of x into a sequence of Parikh vectors.
2. The Paper 4 support schemas define the exact boundary geometries (the 19 families).
3. A global certificate (the parent ancestor theorem for x) guarantees that the unbounded macroscopic sequence of blocks avoids the instantiated affine targets.

### C. Pure Repetition Boundary Rules (aa2fr variant)
If the exact aa2fr language is targeted, any xx pure repetition must be part of an xxx repetition, and all other abelian squares are strictly forbidden. This rule must be integrated into the local finite-factor gate.

### D. Source-Language Assumptions
The source language F(x) is the set of all finite factors of x. 
We strictly assume that G is only applied to F(x). We do NOT require G to map arbitrary combinations from Gamma^* safely.

### E. Target Block-Profile Assumptions
The images G(gamma) are typically restricted to known safe subsets, such as the AFE (Abelian-Square-Free Extendable) positive block sets, which limits the search space to blocks with known favorable continuation properties.
