## 7. Classical theory boundary

Three pieces of outside theory matter immediately.

### Walk partition versus equitable partition

Powers and Sulaiman (1982), *The walk partition and colorations of a graph*, explicitly study the partition induced by numbers of walks issuing from vertices and note that it is not generally an equitable partition.

Therefore:

\[
\text{all-horizon equal walk counts}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{equitable equivalence}
\]

in general.

Our repeated equality of these notions in the Abelian pilots is empirical structure that needs explanation.

### Hankel rank and minimal weighted automata

Carlyle and Paz (1971), and the later weighted-automata literature, identify finite Hankel rank with finite weighted realization; the Hankel rank gives the minimal realization dimension.

Therefore the general “rank = minimal linear counting machine” principle is classical.

Paper 6 must claim novelty only for the Abelian-avoidance structure, construction, compression phenomenon, and certified selected-library application.

### Additive-square projection

Rao and Rosenfeld (2018) formulate additive powers over \(\mathbb Z^d\), prove that \(\mathbb Z^2\) is not uniformly 2-repetitive, and explicitly connect additive and Abelian repetitions via Parikh-vector linear maps.

This is the correct surrounding literature for the profile-projection layer.

## 8. Revised Paper-6 structural conjectures

### Conjecture P6-A — Abelian cutoff walk partition rigidity

For the specific suffix automata induced by equal-length aa2f/aa2fr block extension, all-horizon count equivalence may often be stable/equitable even though this is false for general directed graphs.

**Status:** true in every tested Abelian pilot; no theorem.

The next task is not to assume it but to find the first counterexample or identify a sufficient structural condition.

### Conjecture P6-B — bounded semantic complexity is controlled by obstruction geometry

The large literal suffix space may contain far fewer distinct future-count behaviours because many literal histories induce the same family of Parikh-difference/cutpoint obstruction responses.

Paper 4 should be tested as an explanatory predictor of the 152 → 97 and 34 → 27 collapses, not merely as an implementation optimization.

### Conjecture P6-C — complete-library recoding invariance

For complete block libraries, some semantic dimensions of the block-boundary operator may be inherited from the underlying character-level cutoff system and therefore stabilize across block lengths.

**Status:** observed for L=4,5,6 and Kmax=2,3,4; likely calibration/classical operator structure rather than Paper-6 novelty.
