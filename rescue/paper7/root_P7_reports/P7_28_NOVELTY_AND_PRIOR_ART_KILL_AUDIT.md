## 1. Exact Theorem Statement
Let $\mathcal{A}_4$ be the factorial language of finite Abelian-square-free words over $\Sigma_4 = \{a,b,c,d\}$.
There exists a finite word $s \in \mathcal{A}_4$ such that $s \in re(\mathcal{A}_4) \setminus e(\mathcal{A}_4)$.
Equivalently:
$$re(\mathcal{A}_4) \setminus e(\mathcal{A}_4) \neq \emptyset$$

## 2. Stronger Theorem Formulation
Because our specific witness $s = \text{abacabadc}$ admits exactly 0 valid single-letter left extensions ($L_1(s) = \emptyset$), it follows trivially that $s \notin le(\mathcal{A}_4)$. Since $e(\mathcal{A}_4) \subseteq le(\mathcal{A}_4)$, the theorem establishes the strictly stronger separation:
$$re(\mathcal{A}_4) \setminus le(\mathcal{A}_4) \neq \emptyset$$
This proves that the right-extendable and left-extendable parts of $\mathcal{A}_4$ are not merely non-identical, but their asymmetric difference is non-empty.

## 3. Primary-Source History of the Open Question
Veikko Keränen introduced the concept of "unfavourable factors" (or "forbidden factors") in his computer-aided searches (e.g., 2007 conferences; 2010 *Mathematica Journal*). An unfavourable factor is defined precisely as an Abelian-square-free word that cannot occur as a proper factor inside any bi-infinite ASF word. 
Keränen posed the open question of whether an unfavourable factor could nonetheless be a prefix of a one-sided infinite ASF word. Our theorem answers this directly: $s$ is an unfavourable factor that initiates a right-infinite ASF word. Thus, it is an **EXACT RESOLUTION** (and structurally stronger due to immediate left-death) to Keränen's open question.

## 6. Theorem-Equivalence Analysis
* **Shur's Growth Theorem:** Shur established that $\text{Gr}(L) = \text{Gr}(re(L)) = \text{Gr}(e(L))$. This proves the *growth rates* of the extendable parts are identical, but it does NOT prove $re(L) = e(L)$ as sets. Shur leaves the structural divergence of these sets for specific languages like $\mathcal{A}_4$ unaddressed.
* **Keränen's Searches:** Keränen identified finite unfavourable factors to prune search trees. No primary source establishes a proven right-infinite continuation for any of these pruned dead-ends.
No equivalent existence theorem was located in the literature.
